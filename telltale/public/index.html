Method

How the score is built

There is no AI judging the text. The engine measures surface statistics and blends them with weights that reflect how reliable each one is, then shows its work.

Segment and measure

The text is split into sentences and paragraphs. For each one we measure length, variance, vocabulary, punctuation and phrasing.

Score 14 signals

Each signal converts a measurement into an AI-leaning probability from 0 to 1. Absent evidence never votes — no paragraphs, no paragraph signal.

Weight and blend

A weighted mean produces a raw score. The strongest signals — phrase habits and rhythm — carry the most weight.

Shrink for short text

Below ~180 words the score is pulled toward the middle and flagged low-confidence, because a short sample simply cannot support a strong claim.

Signal weights

Why these weights? Phrase habits and sentence-length variance are the most stable machine tells we found in testing across marketing copy, essays, reports and emails. Punctuation and long-word ratio are noisy, so they barely move the needle.
These are the general-register defaults. When a text is detected (or declared) as academic, legal, technical or journalistic, the profile rescales the signals that legitimately differ in that genre — rhythm stays in play everywhere, because no register explains away a flat rhythm and a diet of stock phrases.
Honesty

What this can and cannot do

It can

  • ✓Recognise the stylistic signature of default, unedited model output: even sentence lengths, stock transitions, hedged generalities.
  • ✓Show you the specific sentences and phrases that produced the suspicion, so you can judge the evidence yourself.
  • ✓Tell you when a document is too short or too uniform to say anything useful.

It cannot

  • ✕Prove authorship. A careful human can write prose that scores high, and a rewriter can launder machine text until it scores low.
  • ✕Detect text that was AI-generated in an earlier draft, then rewritten. It only sees what is in front of it.
  • ✕Justify punishing anyone. A score is a conversation starter, never a verdict. Academic misuse of these tools is a real problem; a single number is not a fair process.
FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Is a high score proof that AI wrote it?

No. It is evidence of style, not authorship. Formal human writing — legal, academic, non-native English — often scores in the suspicious middle. Read the signal breakdown before drawing conclusions.

How accurate is it?

On unedited model output with the usual habits it's usually decisive. On heavily edited or humanised text it degrades quickly — that's a limitation of every stylometric detector, including the commercial ones that claim 99% accuracy.

Do you send my text anywhere?

No. The analysis runs in a single local process on this server. Nothing is sent to an external model or API. Only the scans you run through the app are stored, in data/scans.json, and you can wipe them from the dashboard.

Why did my own writing score as AI?

Usually one of: em dashes, tidy sentence lengths, no contractions, or a corporate/academic register. The report lists every signal it used, and the "What to change" panel tells you which habits to break if you're being flagged unfairly.

Why does formal writing get its own profile?

Because a single global scale misreads genre. A contract full of "shall", "hereby" and "indemnify" is not an AI tell — it's a contract. Register calibration discounts vocabulary that the genre explains and leaves the signals that no genre excuses fully in play. You can see the before/after score on every report, so the adjustment is never hidden.

What about GPTZero and the rest?

Same family of techniques, different packaging. The difference here is that TellTale shows you the entire calculation instead of a proprietary confidence number. You can also run it yourself, which most detectors don't allow.

The five bands

A score of 50 means the evidence is contradictory or thin. That is a real answer, and the honest one for a great deal of content in the wild.
